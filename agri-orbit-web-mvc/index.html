Glossary and method

Evapotranspiration (ET)

Water leaving the field into the air: what evaporates from the soil plus what plants transpire. If a month's rain falls well below ET, the crop suffers. ECOSTRESS measures it from space.

Water balance

Rainfall (plus irrigation) minus evapotranspiration. In the calendar, amber bars mark months when rain doesn't reach even half of what the field loses.

Soil moisture and anomaly

How much water is in the root zone (SMAP). An anomaly is the difference from normal for that time of year: a negative anomaly warns of drought before it shows on the plant.

NDVI (vegetation index)

A number from −1 to 1 computed from the red and infrared light a plant reflects (Landsat, MODIS). High values mean vigorous crops; low, bare soil or stressed plants.

Frost risk

Months when the average minimum temperature drops below about 3 °C: some nights likely reach 0 °C and damage sensitive crops.

Total water storage (GRACE)

GRACE and GRACE-FO measure tiny gravity changes and show the regional anomaly of ALL stored water (surface, soil and aquifers). A sustained decline means more is withdrawn than recharged; it is regional context, not a plot-level figure.

Legumes, cover crops and deep roots

Legumes (beans, faba bean, tarwi, mucuna) fix nitrogen from the air. Cover crops protect the soil and add organic matter. Deep roots (sorghum, sunflower, quinoa) loosen the soil and improve infiltration.

Satellite vs. ground measurement

The satellite sees large areas every day, but in 1–36 km pixels with estimates. A station or soil test measures a single point more precisely. Combining both gives the best result.

How it reaches a recommendation

  1. Harmonizes the data: brings climate (POWER), moisture (SMAP), rainfall (GPM), elevation (SRTM) and groundwater (GRACE) to a single point and to monthly and seasonal values.
  2. Scores the suitability of each crop from temperature, available water, heat, frost, pH and texture.
  3. Evaluates over a thousand rotations and adds the effects between crops: nitrogen left by a legume, pests shared within the same family, water used by a cover crop.
  4. Weights with your priorities (income, soil, water, stability, forage) and discards crops you cannot sell.
  5. Measures uncertainty with 1,000 simulated years of rainfall and temperature, and tells you which data are missing.

Where human judgment is needed: choosing varieties and sowing dates, confirming markets and prices, knowing your plot's pest history and validating the soil in the field.
